## 1. Why

- D-069 (1) says a troll-route section offers several routes, some of which fail: fake or
  collapsing platforms, dead ends, triggered traps. The validator must prove at least one valid
  route.
- KIT-3a built that validator. The kit still lacks:
  - the fake platform;
  - rules for betrayals that don't kill by themselves;
  - a real multi-route section to prove it on.
- **PAX-075 also measured that three claimed betrayals don't happen in the real code:**
  - L004 `FalseLanding`: a full-speed take-off at x ≥ 15.28 skips it, including the natural edge
    jump. The braked landing on it has a release window of about 6 ticks.
  - L004 `Block_1`: no route dies on it.
  - L002 `Block_A`: no route dies on it.

## 2. What to build

1. **The fake platform** (a new element kind). It looks like a `Floor`, but it isn't solid.
   - On the first tick the cat overlaps it, it visibly reveals itself: a renderer or colour change
     that D-079 counts as "visible". The cat falls through.
   - It's deterministic and resets with the room (D-041).
   - Phase 1 proposes the implementation: a new `RoomTrap` kind or a builder-only element. Choose
     whichever is smaller and needs no change to an existing trap.
2. **Non-lethal betrayals in the route validator** (extends D-079). A betrayal route may end in one
   of two ways:
   - **Dies:** at a declared killer (for example the pit's hazard), with the lead measured from
     `revealedBy` (for example the fake platform or collapse), and ≥ 6.
   - **Recovers:** it reaches `RoomComplete()` after the betrayal. This is how a dead end proves
     it isn't a soft-lock (D-053) on its authored path.

   An outcome that is neither of these fails. A cat alive at the tick cap is a failure, and the
   error names it as a possible soft-lock.
3. **Coverage for non-lethal betrayals** (extends D-074), in a separately named rule. The betrayal's
   "danger" is the surface that betrays: the top strip the cat would stand on.
   - A contact-triggered element (a fake platform, or a collapse triggered by standing on it)
     covers itself.
   - A zone-triggered one (for example a Solid that moves away) must satisfy D-074's cut rule
     against that strip.
4. **The troll-route dev room:** Trap Lab room 4, following the room-3 pattern (its own checkpoint
   and door).
   - 10–12 platforms, at least three routes, and exactly one valid route.
   - The failing routes include at least:
     - one fake platform;
     - one collapsing thin platform;
     - one dead end that recovers;
     - one triggered trap, fed by an arrow chain ("the arrow fires, then the floor goes"; D-078
       allows arrows as chain sources).
   - Everything is declared in `TrapLabRoutes` and proven by `ValidateRoutes`, with every jump
     comfortable (D-056 (2)) and every timed window ≥ 12.
   - Size: one screen or close to it (D-040).
5. **The FalseLanding ruling, as data only.** L001–L004 get no layout change: D-069 and PAX-078
   R15 say harder content comes from new levels, not from reworking the seed levels.
   - L004 `FalseLanding` and `Block_1`, and L002 `Block_A`, are recorded in D-080 as "claimed but
     not a betrayal of any route".
   - Their layout comments are corrected. The route files get a comment saying why these have no
     betrayal route.
   - Phase 1 may argue for a layout change instead. That needs my ruling and new §9 play steps.

  methods, never in or named `Validate`.
