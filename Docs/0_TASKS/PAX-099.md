# PAX-099 · KIT-11: angled arrows, repeating arrows, every floor pattern from level 3, doors on platforms

**Status:** Approved 2026-10-02 (the developer: "Yes start"). Runs before PAX-100/101.
**Wait after Phase 1:** post the trace and stop.
**Phase 1 size: Full.** A new runtime kill shape (an angled arrow) and a new no-pass-through proof.
**Depends on:** nothing open. PAX-A16 (art) is paused, uncommitted, until PAX-099–101 are done (the developer: "gameplay
first"). **Decisions:** D-078 (the arrow), D-086 (the spear), D-057 (6-tick lead), D-083 (camera tell), D-091 (rewind),
D-065/D-085 (band 1), D-095 (floors that move). New **D-106**, written as built.

## 1. Why

The developer (2026-10-02), after playing: "arrows fire only once. I was expecting the arrows to fire multiple times at
an interval. Interval can change from level to level. … it always fires straight. You should have them fire at multiple
angles as well (diagonally). The point is variety and certain amount of challenge." And: "Add moving platforms and moving
floors, floors that go down and shift while you're standing … these are classic in Level Devil and are still missing."

Rulings (the developer, 2026-10-02):
- Angled arrows at **several angles**, from **level 3**. Amends D-078 ("horizontal only").
- **Every floor pattern** (mover, slide-away, drop-and-return/sink, shrinker, push wall) is allowed in **levels 3–10**,
  with band 1's forgiving slack. Amends D-095 (3) and D-065's band split for floors. L001–L002 stay as they are.
- Floors to use: riders, sink while standing, slide while standing. No new "blink" floor.
- **Doors stand on a platform** (the developer, with a screenshot of a door beside an inverted cat: "Exits just hanging in
  the air doesn't make sense here. Especially if it is inverted. Keep the door in the same direction but maybe just keep
  it on a platform." By exit they mean the door). D-105's plinth is art only and doesn't answer it: the platform must
  be real geometry.

## 2. What to build

1. **Angled arrows.** An arrow (not a spear: D-086 stays horizontal) declares an angle from a fixed set:
   0° (today), ±30°, ±45°, ±60° from horizontal, firing left or right. Same tell, flight and stop as D-078, as a pure
   function of ticks along the angled lane. It stops at the lane's end face in fixed geometry and is harmless from then.
   The kill test uses the arrow's rotated box, and the route harness names the killer with the same function.
2. **Repeating arrows.** Periodic and Rearm arrows exist (D-078 uses the existing repeat modes; `ValidateArrowCooldown`,
   `ValidateArrowPeriodicSlack`) but no level uses one. Confirm with a fixture that a Periodic arrow fires again every
   `PeriodTicks`, snaps back to the mouth, and rewinds exactly. Fix it if not. The interval is per element (per level).
3. **Validators:** the angled lane (an oriented box from the mouth to the end face) ends in fixed geometry, crosses no
   moving Solid's swept path and no `CollapsingFloor` (D-078's limits), and keeps door clearance; the speed cap holds
   against a cat moving in both axes (run and fall speed), or the angled arrow gets its own cap; the camera tell (D-083)
   sees the launcher and the first lethal pose at 4:3, 16:9 and 20:9; band 1's rules accept angled and repeating arrows
   from level 3; `ValidateMovingFloors` allows every pattern from level 3.
4. **Art:** `ArrowArt` turns the arrow (and its trail) to the angle; the launcher's mouth faces it. The disguised
   launcher still matches its host (P10).
5. **Doors on platforms.** Every door whose bottom doesn't rest on fixed geometry (today L004, L009, L010; the validator
   lists them) gets a real solid platform under it, in the layout data: a short `Floor` (or slab) whose top is the door's
   bottom, at least as wide as the door. The door keeps its position and stays upright, in every gravity. The platform
   is dressed by the normal skin rules, so D-105's art-only plinth (`RoomSetup.BuildDoorPlinth` and its call in
   `SoloRoomBuilder`) is removed. Each changed level's solution and betrayal routes still replay as before, or the
   change is reported.
6. **Trap Lab:** room 3 (or a new room) gets one arrow at each angle and one Periodic arrow, each with a solution and a
   betrayal route.

## 7. Phase 1 trace (2026-10-02)

**Q1, the kill shape.** Today the kill test (`RoomTrap.IsLocalHumanOverlapping`) and the harness (`RouteReplay.Overlaps`)
are both `Physics2D.OverlapBox(centre, size, 0f)` on `ArrowTrap.TryGetKillBox`. An angled arrow uses the same call with
the lane angle: `TryGetKillBox` also returns the angle, `RoomTrap` gets an overload with an angle, and the harness passes
it too. Killer naming stays a single function. The Box2D skin note (D-078) is unchanged, because the skin doesn't depend
on the rotation. **Needs `RoomTrap.cs` added to the allowed list** (one overload; no other trap changes).

**Q2, the speed cap.** D-078's cap, `v ≤ L + (w − h) − 2·run`, counts only motion along the lane, with the cat
running. For an angled lane, the cat's speed along the lane is up to `run·cosθ + fall·sinθ`, with run 0.12 u/tick and
fall 0.4 u/tick (`maxFallSpeed` 20 u/s). The cat's chord along the lane, through the arrow's band, replaces `w − h`:
`v ≤ L + chord(θ) − 2·(run·cosθ + fall·sinθ)`, as a pure `ArrowMath` function. It reduces to D-078 exactly at 0°.
A rough estimate for an 0.8 arrow at 45° is a cap of about 0.5 u/tick, against the default 0.3. A brute-force test
fixes the numbers in Phase 2: it sweeps cat velocities and offsets at each angle at the cap and finds no pass-through
outside a 0.05 u graze. A diagonal graze stays a known limit, as in D-078.

**Q3, the lane data.** `ArrowLane` gets `AngleDegrees` (default 0), in {0, ±30, ±45, ±60}; positive is up, measured
from the fire direction. The mouth stays on the launcher's side face at `LaneY`. `LaneEndX` is still where the tip
stops. Travel along the lane is `|LaneEndX − mouthX| / cosθ − L`. The validator checks the lane as an oriented box
(separating axes) against fixed solids, and checks that the tip's stop point lies on a fixed solid's face (within 1e-3)
or at the room's end. The swept-path, door-clearance and frame checks use the lane's bounding box (conservative). The
builder rotates the arrow child; `ArrowTrap.LocalPose` moves it along `(sign·cosθ, sinθ)`.

**Q4, camera tell and chaos.** Both read the arrow renderer's bounds from the replay record. A rotated sprite gives its
axis-aligned box, slightly larger at the corners (worst case about 0.3 u for an 0.8 arrow at 45°), so "visible" could be
claimed a little early. This is measured in Phase 2 on the Trap Lab room. If it matters, the check tests the arrow's
centre instead.

**Q5, repeating arrows.** These already work. `TrapTiming` Periodic and Rearm drive `ArrowTrap`, rearm calls
`ShowUnfired`, and a rewind restores the pose from ticks. Trap Lab room 3's `ArrowA` is Periodic 120 and is tested
(`ArrowTrapRuntimeTests`, `ArrowValidatorTests`). "Fires only once" is because no level uses Periodic or Rearm; the
interval per level is just `PeriodTicks`. Two gaps, both fixed in Phase 2:
- `ValidateArrowCooldown` doesn't reject `cooldown ≥ period`, which would silently skip every other shot.
- The periodic-slack crossing assumes a horizontal lane: the cat runs its whole length. An angled lane's crossing is its
  footprint on the walk band.

**Q6, doors (room auditor's box-model predictions; `ValidateRoutes` decides).**
- **L004:** the door also retreats, from x 6.5–7.1 to x 2.2–2.8. A fixed shelf `Door_Shelf` at (4.65, 13.25), 4.9 × 0.5,
  lies under both poses and the whole slide. Every route is unchanged. A cat that stands still in Flip_E now rests
  under the shelf, and walking off either end goes on as before (no soft-lock, no shortcut).
- **L009:** a fixed `Door_Ledge` at (3, 12.25), 1.0 × 0.5. Every route is unchanged, and the ledge adds no landing.
  The auditor flagged a *pre-existing* possible bypass: a cat that jumps into the floating Flip_D1 while moving left may
  rise past Slab_D to the roof and walk to the door. I'll add it as a declared dead-end route and replay it. If it
  completes the room, the fix is L009 layout work (PAX-100), not this ticket.
- **L010:** the door retreats right, over the solution's last jump. A fixed shelf under both poses cuts the jump short,
  so the cat lands on Roof_6 and dies. The fix is all fixed geometry: `Pad_A` (17.8, 12.25), 1.0 × 0.5, under the
  authored pose, and `Pad_B` (23.35, 12.25), 1.3 × 0.5, flush with Wall_R, under a retreated pose. That needs the
  **retreat offset changed from (3.5, 0) to (5.0, 0)**, because at 3.5 the jump's arc bumps a pad there. The
  solution's last jump is marked `.Timed`; its window shrinks from about 27 to about 17 ticks, still ≥ 12. T6 ("follow
  the door") is unchanged. The other option, a pad that moves with the door, is a new runtime element (a moving Solid
  under D-056 (3)) and still bumps at 3.5.
- **The validator rule:** at the door's authored pose, and at its retreated pose if it has one, a fixed Floor, Wall or
  Ceiling's top is within 1e-4 of the door's bottom and covers the door's x range. L004, L009 and L010 fail before the
  fix.

**Other findings**
- `ValidateMovingFloors` requires an explicit Carry or Slip on sideways Solids only from level 14. With movers allowed
  from level 3, the threshold becomes 3. No existing element changes: the only Legacy Solid below 14 is L008's
  vertical `Lift_3`.
- **A16 overlap:** D-105's door plinth (`RoomSetup.BuildDoorPlinth` and its call in `SoloRoomBuilder`) is uncommitted
  A16 work. Removing it here leaves both files at HEAD apart from this ticket's own changes. D-105's door bullet is
  replaced by D-106. At commit time only this ticket's hunks are staged (`git apply --cached`).
- **Test gaps (not in scope; noted for a later ticket):** no general headroom check that a required jump's arc is free
  of fixed solids (the L010 shelf failure is this case), and no rule that every floating flip is entered from both
  sides on a dead-end route.

**Rulings needed**

3. L009's possible bypass: check it here, and fix it in PAX-100 if it's real (recommended).
