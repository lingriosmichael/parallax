## 1. The developer's brief (2026-09-25)

- **Hard, but not impossible.** Levels 1–5 have **at least 5 deadly traps** each, and levels 6–10 **at least 6–7**, so a first-time player dies many times in every level.
- **These ten levels are the hook** (D-068): they're free, and they must be challenging and fun enough that the player pays to unlock the rest.
- **Progression through skill and memory.** Each level teaches something; later levels demand that you remember earlier lessons and combine them.
- **No clues, ever.** Nothing marks where a trap is: no cracks, signs, colours or odd shapes. A trap reveals itself only when it triggers, and it still moves visibly for at least 6 ticks before it can kill (D-057), so every death shows its cause.
- **Hard means troll and memory, not execution.** Jumps and timing stay at band 1's forgiving numbers (reach 0.75, windows ≥ 12). Tight execution starts at level 11 (D-065).
- **L001–L004 are redesigned** to this standard (they were about 1.5 deaths each). Levels 5–10 are new.

## 2. What to build

### 2.1 The content rules (checked, and recorded as D-085)

For every numbered level 1–10:
1. **Deadly traps (lethal betrayals):**
   - levels 1–5: **at least 5**;
   - levels 6–7: **at least 6**;
   - levels 8–10: **at least 7**.

   Each is declared as a Dies route with its own killer, and each lead is ≥ 6. Most of them lie **in sequence** on the way to the door: at least 4 in levels 1–5, and at least 5 in levels 6–10. Each of these betrayal routes starts by surviving all the earlier ones (it uses their learned answers, then falls for the next). That's the checkable stand-in for "dies once per trap the first time". The rest may sit on tempting side routes.
2. **One solution route**, with every window ≥ 12, reach 0.75, and no precision sections (the band check).
3. **No tells.** Before its reveal, every trap element looks like the surface or background it sits in (§3.1 lists each kind). A falling block that visibly hangs in the air, or a launcher in a different colour, counts as a clue and isn't allowed.
4. **Size:** 15–40 s of play once known. That's longer than D-040's 10–20 s, to fit 5–7 traps. A room may be **taller than it is wide**, for levels that climb up or down, and the camera then follows vertically. The width stays within today's frame (about 33), unless a sketch shows it can't fit its traps (then §4 (c)). The camera tell rule (D-083) must pass at every aspect. D-085 records this, since D-040/D-069 only allowed oversized rooms for precision.
5. **Every existing rule passes:** coverage, surface coverage, door clearance, and the rest.
6. **Direction variety (the developer's brief).** Levels don't always run left to right. The start and the door can be anywhere: the middle, the right, the top or the bottom. The route can climb, descend, or double back.
   - **Per level:** the door is far from the start, at least half the room's width or half its height away. It's never reachable by the obvious straight line.
   - **Across levels 1–10:**
     - at most 3 levels run plainly left to right;
     - at least 2 climb up;
     - at least 2 go down;
     - at least 3 start in the middle or on the right;
     - at least 2 double back (the route heads away from the door first).

A new, separately named rule, `ValidateBand1Content`, checks items 1, 2, 4 and 6 (per level) for levels 1–10. A test over all ten levels checks item 6's mix (it runs after half B). Item 3 is checked per element kind by §3.1's findings, plus a test for each kind that can be disguised.

### 2.2 The progression (the Architect's draft; the developer can change any line in §9 step 0)

Each level has **one new lesson**, and from level 6 on, it **turns an earlier lesson against you**. The last column is the minimum number of deadly traps (§2.1 item 1).

| Lvl | Start → door | Lesson (what the player learns) | Main kit | Betrayals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | left → right, flat | The floor lies: the obvious run gets you killed; hesitate and watch | CollapsingFloor, falling block | 5 |
| 2 | bottom right → top right, **doubles back** (Phase 1 ruling) | Look up: running under things is deadly; stop, bait it, then go | ceiling spikes, falling block, door retreat | 5 |
| 3 | bottom left → top right, **climb up** a staircase | Not all ground is ground: the obvious step is fake; the real way is less obvious | FakePlatform, pit | 5 |
| 4 | **middle** → door on the ceiling, left | Gravity: a flip that looks like an escape drops you onto hidden spikes | gravity Flip, hidden spikes | 5 |
| 5 | top right → bottom left, **climb down** a zigzag staircase | The walls shoot: a jump or a drop fires a disguised launcher; wait, or take the other ledge | Arrow (disguised) | 5 |
| 6 | **middle** → right, but you must go left first (**doubles back**) | Rhythm: periodic spikes and a sweep; count, then commit. L1's "hesitate" now kills (a delayed chain punishes standing still) | periodic spikes, sweep, chained block | 6 |
| 7 | bottom right → top left, **climb up**, zigzag | Trust nothing: the step that was fake in L3 is real here, and the "safe" step is the trap | FakePlatform, CollapsingFloor, pit | 6 |
| 8 | top left → bottom right, **go down** | Chains: one trigger sets off a delayed sequence down the room; memorise the order | chains, falling blocks, sliding spikes | 7 |
| 9 | right, middle height → top left | Both ways: gravity plus arrows; the flip you learned in L4 fires an arrow lane | Flip, Arrow, hidden spikes | 7 |
| 10 | **middle** → a door right next to the start, behind a wall; the route loops the whole room (**doubles back**) | The exam: every lesson once, in a new order; the door itself retreats last | mixed, door retreat | 7 |

This mix meets §2.1 item 6: only levels 1 and 8 run left to right, levels 3 and 7 climb, levels 5 and 8 descend, 7 levels start in the middle or on the right, and levels 2, 6, 7 and 10 double back.

Deaths should rise from at least 5 on level 1 to at least 7 on level 10. Each lesson in the table is the level's **theme**: the other traps in the level reuse earlier lessons, so memory load grows level by level. The solution always exists, and it's always learnable from the deaths before it.

## 4. Outcomes, decided now

- **(a) Everything passes.** Pin it, and go on (half A → stop for the developer's play → half B).
- **(b) A kit kind can't be made tell-free without runtime code.** Don't use that kind in 1–10. Report it and propose a kit-gap ticket. No stop, unless a §2.2 lesson depends on it: then stop and propose a substitute lesson.
- **(c) A sketch can't meet §2.1** (its trap count and sequence within the size, with windows ≥ 12, and its start and door). Report it with the closest variant. The Architect rules; no code for that level until then.
- **(e) A direction or shape assumption from §3.5 blocks a level** (for example the camera can't follow a tall room, or a rule only works left to right). If the fix is Editor-only (a rule or the builder), propose it and wait for a ruling. If it needs runtime code, stop and propose a kit-gap ticket. Meanwhile, re-plan that level's direction within what works.
- **(d) A level needs a new element or behaviour.** Stop that level and propose a kit-gap ticket. Other levels go on.

## 5. Rules

- **No runtime code**, except a builder-only disguise (the builder, TrapKitSetup, and the element's colour/sorting at build time). Anything else is §4 (b) or (d).
- **Band 1 thresholds only.** No precision sections, and no bait gap used as a required element.
- L001–L004's scenes, routes and pins change; that's expected. Trap Lab rooms and every validator threshold don't.
- Tests are deterministic only. Tests that open scenes restore the Test Runner's scene (CLAUDE.md).
- **Don't share level spoilers in the console or scene names.** Element names are neutral ("Floor_3", not "FakeFloor").
