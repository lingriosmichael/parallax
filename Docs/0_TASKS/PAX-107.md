    end (Step_A, Step_B), or give the stair a new dead end. Fill the run from the new start with moving-platform play.
- **B3. More creative moving platforms** (a proposal; Phase 1 sketches it with numbers):

  - Every one keeps a betrayal route and band-1's rules (window ≥ 12, solution 500–1500 ticks, dead ends that kill).

### Part C · L009: a new spike puzzle (brainstorm, then the developer picks)

The developer: "Currently a bit too easy. Its not really about arrows … the design seems somewhat repetitive of another
earlier level … Level 9 needs something new, something creative. Something that hasn't been done at all in any of the
levels. I need a sort of puzzle that retracts or protrudes spikes, lets brainstorm on this."

- *Why it feels repetitive:* L009 has the same shape as L004 and L010. You run one way along S1, take a floor flip up at
  its end, and walk back along the roof upside down, past flips that fire arrows. Three of the ten band-1 levels share
  that loop.
- *Already in the kit:* hidden spikes (once, rearm, periodic, chained), falling blocks, collapsing and fake floors,
  arrows and spears, gravity flips, inverters, geysers, vines, storm clouds, movers, shrinking floors, push walls, the
  crush ledge, the hinge floor and grip walls.
- *Not yet in the kit:* nothing the player **switches**, and nothing that reacts to **what the player does** (rather than
  where the cat stands).

The options below are all spike mechanics nobody has seen in PARALLAX. Each is deterministic and tick-based, so it fits
D-040:

1. **Spike switches (recommended).** Pressure plates on the floor flip spike banks. Plate A retracts bank 1 and raises
   bank 2; plate B does the reverse. The room is a small order puzzle: stand on the plates in the right order to open a
   path. The troll: one plate also raises spikes under where the cat just came from. New trap kind (a plate with up to
   two linked spike banks; the banks restore from the plates' state, so rewinds are exact).
2. **Jump-parity spikes.** Every jump the cat makes swaps two spike sets (red up/blue down ↔ blue up/red down). Crossing
   the room means planning your jumps: an even or odd number of hops lands you on the safe set. Very readable (two colours)
   and impossible in any other level. New trap kind reading the cat's jump count.
3. **Footprint spikes.** Spikes rise wherever the cat stood a set number of ticks ago (a trail chasing it). Backtracking
   kills, and stopping is fine only for a moment. The room is a one-way snake puzzle through a maze of floors.
4. **Spike wave.** A long floor whose spike segments rise and retract in a travelling wave (left to right, like a stadium
   wave). The cat rides the gap in the wave, then must stop dead as a second wave comes the other way. Builds on periodic
   hidden spikes with phase offsets (cheapest; no new kind), but less "new" than 1–3.
5. **Mirror spikes.** Floor spikes and the ceiling spikes over them are linked: when one set retracts, the other
   protrudes. With L009's gravity flips, the safe surface is always the one you're *not* on; flip at the right moment.
   Pairs well with L009's existing flips; new linked-pair behaviour on hidden spikes.

**Stop before Part C:** the developer picks one (or a mix), and whether L009 keeps its flip loop or gets a new layout.
Then Phase 1 for Part C (Full if a new trap kind).
