## 1. Why

- D-065 makes level 11 onward extremely hard. D-069 allows precision there.
- Before any hard level is authored (Phase D), the kit needs five things:
  - a way to mark a precision section;
  - tighter thresholds applied only inside it;
  - a check that keeps precision out of levels 1–10;
  - a bait gap that the validator proves can't be crossed;
  - proof on a dev room that the rules work.
- **Wider rooms bring a camera risk.** D-069's precision rooms can be wider than one screen, and L001–L004 already run in follow mode below about a 20:9 aspect ratio (D-071 (4)).
  - In follow mode, a trap can reveal off screen and kill before the player could see it.
  - D-071 (6) and D-078 made that the validator's job, and deferred it to here.

## 2. What to build

1. **The section marker.** A precision section is marked in layout data.
   - **Default:** a region element (an x-range and y-range inside one room).
   - A `RequiredJump` is a precision jump if its take-off *and* its landing are both inside the region.
   - A jump that straddles the region's edge (the entry and exit jumps) is checked with D-056's values (0.75, 12). It isn't an error: every section has to be entered and left.
   - Timing cases (§2.3) belong to a section only if they are wholly inside it. Otherwise D-056 applies.
   - Phase 1 may propose a different representation (§3.1).
2. **One config asset:** `PrecisionThresholds` (a ScriptableObject).
   - Its fields:
     - the reach fraction;
     - the timing slack in ticks.
   - **Provisional values:** reach **0.85**, slack **8** ticks. They're tighter than D-056's 0.75 and 12, and still strictly inside the contract with positive slack (D-069 (2)(a)).
   - The developer may change them in §9 before the commit, within 0.75 ≤ reach < 1.0 and 0 < slack ≤ 12.
   - PAX-069 replaces them with device numbers.
   - D-083 amends D-069's "thresholds come from a Pixel 8a session": now it's provisional values here, and device values in PAX-069.
3. **The validator:**
   - Inside a marked section, reach and timing slack use the config.
   - Outside a section, everything is unchanged (D-056).
   - Leads stay at ≥ 6 everywhere (D-057 and D-069 (2)(b)).
   - The reach check uses `JumpReach` with the section's fraction. No second copy of the math.
   - A missing config is a validator error, not a skip, when a section exists.
4. **The band check (D-065):**
   - A precision marker in a level numbered 1–10 is a validator error that names the level.
   - Trap Lab rooms are exempt, because they aren't numbered levels.
   - The level number comes from where Phase 1 says (§3.2). It isn't hard-coded per layout.
5. **The Trap Lab precision room** (room 5, next after room 4):
   - 8–12 platforms, narrow and thin (the PAX-073 thin-platform setup), inside one marked section;
   - at least one trap or arrow mixed in (D-069 (2)(b));
   - it may be wider than one screen (D-069 room size), so the camera follows;
   - a solution route plus at least one lethal betrayal, proven by `ValidateRoutes`;
   - **at least one required jump that passes at the precision fraction and fails at 0.75.** That proves the marker matters. Seen red: remove the marker, and the room fails.
   - **one bait gap (§2.7)** with its missing step, built from existing elements only: a way round, or a visible platform that's easy to overlook. A platform that appears when triggered would be a new element type (§8). A betrayal route attempts the gap and dies.
6. **The camera tell rule.** It applies to every room in follow mode, at 4:3, 16:9 and 20:9.
   - For every reveal in every declared route (solution and betrayals), the reveal's visible change must be inside the camera view for at least D-057's 6 ticks before it can first kill.
   - "Inside the view" means the changed element's bounds, at its pose that tick, overlap the view rectangle. The view comes from the same camera math the game uses (dead zone, look-ahead, the smoothing lag, the clamp to the frame), stepped along the route's recorded cat positions.
   - A room in fit mode at an aspect passes trivially at that aspect.
   - **The rule reuses the game's camera math.** It doesn't copy it (§3.4).
7. **The bait gap** (the developer's design, 2026-09-24).
   - A room may show a gap nobody can cross. The death teaches the player that a step is missing. The validator proves the gap really can't be crossed; otherwise a skilled player skips the puzzle (L004 FalseLanding's edge-jump bypass is the warning).
   - **Element:** a bait gap in layout data: the take-off platform and the target.
   - **Rule:** with `JumpReach` at fraction **1.0** (the cat's true maximum), from the best take-off (full speed, from the very edge, every coyote tick used), the target is out of reach by at least **one run tick of distance** (MaxSpeed × tick, as D-075 (5)). Otherwise it's a validator error that names the gap.
   - Allowed in every level, both bands: it's a puzzle, not an execution test.
   - The solution route may not use a bait gap.
